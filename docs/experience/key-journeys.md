# Key human journeys

## Status and framing

These are intended emotional journeys based on maintainer-stated product intent,
not observations of external users. The observed feeling is currently unknown;
customer interviews and task observation must validate the maps.

The journeys are deliberately independent of implementation. An editor
workbench, notebook, browser app, colleague, or coding agent may support a
journey, but none of those channels is the journey itself.

Valence ranges from -2 (frustrated or anxious) to +2 (confident or curious).
Interventions appear only at emotional cliffs or where the intended feeling is
especially easy to violate.

## Shared journey backbone

**Encounter a school or work question → try a graph perspective → sense a hunch
→ frame an inquiry → explore relationships → challenge the signal → preserve
meaning → share or continue.**

## Journey 1: Try graph data analysis for school or work

**Human job:** learn whether graph analysis is useful for a real assignment,
research question, or workplace problem without making a large commitment first.

| Step | Valence | Energy | Agency | Intended feeling / observed evidence | Intervention at cliffs or mismatches |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Encounter a question involving relationships, influence, paths, groups, or connected events | -1 | Medium | Medium | Intended: curious but unsure where to begin. Observed: unvalidated. | Describe graph analysis through recognizable questions rather than specialist vocabulary. |
| Wonder whether graph analysis is appropriate or too advanced | -1 | Medium | Low | Intended: invited to test the fit without embarrassment. Observed: unvalidated. | Make the trial bounded and state what the person will understand by the end. |
| Explore a credible example and connect it to the school or work question | 0 | High | Medium | Intended: oriented and able to form a mental model. Observed: unvalidated. | The example must explain why relationships change the answer, not merely display an impressive graph. |
| Try one small question using familiar or analogous data | +1 | High | High | Intended: capable and actively learning. Observed: unvalidated. | Provide interpretation alongside the result so success is more than producing output. |
| Discover one insight that would be difficult to see in rows alone | +2 | High | High | Intended: earned surprise and relevance. Observed: unvalidated. | — |
| Decide whether to continue, learn more, use another method, or explain the approach to someone else | +1 | Medium | High | Intended: informed and autonomous. Observed: unvalidated. | Make every decision legitimate; do not frame declining graph analysis as failure. |

**Arc:** uncertain relevance → safe trial → growing competence → earned insight
→ informed choice.

**Peak:** the learner sees how relationships reveal something useful about
their actual school or work question. **Ending:** a confident decision about
whether graph analysis belongs in the work, plus a clear next learning step if
it does.

## Journey 2: Turn a hunch into a useful line of inquiry

**Human job:** make an ambiguous feeling about the data concrete enough to
investigate without demanding a perfect hypothesis up front.

| Step | Valence | Energy | Agency | Intended feeling / observed evidence | Intervention at cliffs or mismatches |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Notice an anomaly, relationship, or unanswered question | -1 | Medium | Medium | Intended: intrigued but unsettled. Observed: unvalidated. | — |
| Decide whether a graph perspective could reveal something useful | -1 | Medium | Low | Intended: cautiously hopeful. Observed: unvalidated. | Explain the kind of question the workbench can help explore without requiring graph expertise. |
| Bring together the relevant material and context | 0 | High | Medium | Intended: oriented and in control. Observed: unvalidated. | Make ownership, location, and scope of the working material unmistakable. |
| Form a first question that is good enough to try | 0 | High | High | Intended: permitted to explore rather than perform certainty. Observed: unvalidated. | Avoid language that implies the first question must be technically or scientifically perfect. |
| Explore relationships and encounter a meaningful pattern | +2 | High | High | Intended: surprised, capable, and curious. Observed: unvalidated. | — |
| Turn the pattern into a sharper next question | +2 | Medium | High | Intended: momentum with direction. Observed: unvalidated. | Keep the next investigative move visible so success does not become a dead end. |

**Arc:** unsettled curiosity → cautious orientation → agency → discovery →
directed curiosity.

**Peak:** a pattern changes how the analyst sees the original question.
**Ending:** not “analysis complete,” but a sharper question worth pursuing.

## Journey 3: Turn an interesting signal into defensible evidence

**Human job:** decide whether a promising pattern deserves belief, further
testing, or rejection.

| Step | Valence | Energy | Agency | Intended feeling / observed evidence | Intervention at cliffs or mismatches |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Feel excitement about an apparent signal | +1 | High | High | Intended: energized but not prematurely certain. Observed: unvalidated. | Do not celebrate a pattern as a conclusion before it has been challenged. |
| Ask what the entities, relationships, and measures actually mean | 0 | High | Medium | Intended: constructively skeptical. Observed: unvalidated. | Make ontology and analytical meaning available at the point of doubt. |
| Trace where the evidence came from and what status it carries | -1 | High | Medium | Intended: supported through necessary friction. Observed: unvalidated. | Prevent provenance and epistemic context from becoming an expert-only scavenger hunt. |
| Try alternative explanations, methods, or slices | 0 | High | High | Intended: intellectually in control. Observed: unvalidated. | Preserve prior attempts so challenge feels cumulative rather than destructive. |
| Decide how confident to be and where uncertainty remains | +1 | Medium | High | Intended: sober confidence. Observed: unvalidated. | Make limitations expressible; do not force a false success/failure binary. |
| Preserve the reasoning and evidence behind the conclusion | +2 | Low | High | Intended: secure and accountable. Observed: unvalidated. | Ensure the chain can survive closing the current surface or changing tools. |

**Arc:** excitement → doubt → disciplined challenge → grounded confidence.

**Peak:** the analyst can explain why the signal is credible—or why it is not.
**Ending:** a bounded claim with visible evidence, method, and uncertainty.

## Journey 4: Bring another mind into the investigation

**Human job:** gain perspective or assistance without losing context,
authorship, or accountability.

| Step | Valence | Energy | Agency | Intended feeling / observed evidence | Intervention at cliffs or mismatches |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Realize the investigation would benefit from another person or tool | 0 | Medium | High | Intended: open, not defeated. Observed: unvalidated. | — |
| Decide what context, evidence, and uncertainty must travel | -1 | High | Medium | Intended: deliberate rather than burdened. Observed: unvalidated. | Make the investigation's state legible enough that handoff is not a reconstruction project. |
| Invite collaboration or delegate a bounded task | 0 | Medium | High | Intended: in control of scope. Observed: unvalidated. | Consequential changes need explicit boundaries and human accountability. |
| The collaborator reconstructs the question and current evidence | -1 | Medium | Low | Intended: quickly oriented. Observed: unvalidated. | This is the critical trust cliff: shared context must not depend on private or invisible state. |
| Compare interpretations and reconcile changes | +1 | High | High | Intended: productively challenged. Observed: unvalidated. | Preserve disagreement and provenance instead of flattening everything into the latest output. |
| Continue with a richer shared understanding | +2 | Medium | High | Intended: supported and still the author of the work. Observed: unvalidated. | — |

**Arc:** openness → handoff anxiety → shared orientation → productive tension →
collective confidence.

**Peak:** another mind adds a useful perspective without first needing a verbal
tour of hidden state. **Ending:** the investigation is richer and remains
accountable.

## Journey 5: Return to an unfinished investigation

**Human job:** recover the thread of thought after time away and continue
without repeating work.

| Step | Valence | Energy | Agency | Intended feeling / observed evidence | Intervention at cliffs or mismatches |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Stop because of interruption, uncertainty, or competing work | -1 | Low | Medium | Intended: safely paused rather than abandoned. Observed: unvalidated. | Preserve state continuously; do not make “save the journey” a final chore. |
| Return without remembering every prior decision | -1 | Low | Low | Intended: welcomed and reassured. Observed: unvalidated. | Show what was being explored, what changed, and what remained unresolved. |
| Reconstruct the strongest evidence and open questions | 0 | Medium | Medium | Intended: reoriented without embarrassment. Observed: unvalidated. | Avoid forcing the analyst to infer meaning from filenames or raw history alone. |
| Choose a thread to resume, revise, or discard | +1 | Medium | High | Intended: decisive and in control. Observed: unvalidated. | — |
| Make progress and leave a clearer marker for the next return | +2 | Medium | High | Intended: cumulative mastery. Observed: unvalidated. | End with a meaningful next question, not only a record of the last command run. |

**Arc:** interruption → disorientation → recognition → agency → continuity.

**Peak:** the analyst recognizes the prior line of thought and sees the next
move. **Ending:** the investigation feels cumulative rather than repeatedly
restarted.

## Channel implications, not separate journeys

- The guided workbench, notebook, and browser dashboard are different ways to
  investigate and challenge a hunch. They should preserve the same questions,
  evidence, and meaning rather than create competing product realities.
- A user-owned project directory is the shared container for continuity and
  collaboration. Explicit paths support the journey because they preserve
  agency; the path itself is not the journey.
- Coding agents and colleagues enter the collaboration journey. They need
  inspectable context and bounded authority, while the human retains
  accountability.
- Saved queries, results, visualizations, exports, provenance, and epistemic
  status matter when they help someone remember, challenge, explain, or share
  the investigation—not merely because the code can produce them.

## Validation priorities

1. Observe students and workplace evaluators trying graph analysis against a
   real question; learn what makes the trial feel relevant rather than tutorial-like.
2. Test whether a first useful insight helps them explain when graph analysis is
   and is not appropriate.
3. Observe how analysts move from a vague hunch to their first testable
   question; current product language may be too graph-centric.
4. Identify the real trust cliff between “interesting” and “credible,” including
   which provenance and uncertainty cues analysts seek first.
5. Test a cold handoff: can a collaborator understand the question, evidence,
   and unresolved threads without a live explanation?
6. Test a return after several days: can the original analyst recover intent,
   not just reopen files?
7. Ask which channel analysts naturally choose at each point, without treating
   their tool preference as their identity.
